# Cross-cutting checks

These apply to every document. Scale expectations to the document: a short runbook needs a light touch, a service design package needs depth.

## How to judge each area

**Value and outcomes (XC-01 to XC-03)**
Look for who the consumers are, what outcome they get, and how the document's work contributes. "Improve efficiency" with no measure is Partly at best.

**Guiding principles (XC-04 to XC-06)**
Do not look for the principle names. Look for the behaviour: does the document build on what exists, work in small steps with feedback, keep things simple, involve the right people, consider the whole system, and automate where it makes sense? Name the principle in your finding only to explain the gap.

**Four dimensions (XC-07)**
Check the document considers all four: organisations and people; information and technology; partners and suppliers; value streams and processes. A purely technical plan with no people or supplier view is a common gap.

**Governance and risk (XC-08, XC-09)**
Who decides, who approves, how risk and compliance are handled. In a regulated setting, missing approvals or audit trail is high priority.

**AI and automation (XC-10)**
If the document uses or proposes AI or automation, check for human oversight, data quality, accountability and how outputs are checked. If it does not, mark N/A unless there is an obvious manual task that should be automated – then raise it as Partly.

**Measures and improvement (XC-11, XC-12)**
Look for named measures with targets or baselines, and a way to review and improve.

## Priority

- **High** – a gap that could cause failure, harm, non-compliance or wasted investment
- **Medium** – a gap that weakens the document but would not stop it working
- **Low** – nice to have, wording or clarity
